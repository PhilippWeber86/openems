= Controller SHI Heat Pump

Runs a Novelan / Alpha Innotec (SHI) heat pump preferably on PV surplus and protects the battery for the household.

== Overview

This Controller is designed for setups where the heat pump has its own, cheaper electricity tariff: PV and battery should serve the household first (household electricity is more expensive), and the heat pump may only be supplied by them when the forecast guarantees that the household stays covered.

The Controller supports both wiring topologies via the `Heat pump position` configuration. The Meter-Type of the heat pump device must be configured to match (`BEHIND_GRID_METER` -> `CONSUMPTION_METERED`, `GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER` -> `CONSUMPTION_NOT_METERED`); the Controller raises the `MeterTypeMismatch` warning otherwise.

* **`BEHIND_GRID_METER`** (default, standard installation — grid meter at the grid connection point): The heat pump is part of the grid measurement, so the Balancing Controller would serve it from the battery by default. This Controller therefore *limits* ESS discharge (`SetActivePowerLessOrEquals`) to the household demand plus the allowed support power. The surplus calculation adds the current heat-pump power back, so the heat pump does not eat its own surplus signal. If the `PredictorManager` provides a prediction for the heat-pump channel (add e.g. `heatPump0/ActivePower` to the predictor's channel addresses), it is subtracted from the consumption prediction for the night reserve; otherwise the reserve stays conservative.
* **`GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER`**: The heat pump is connected upstream of the grid meter; its consumption is invisible at the balancing point and the battery never serves it by default (fail-safe by wiring). PV surplus leaving the segment (grid export) physically flows through the heat-pump branch. While support is allowed, the Controller *forces* battery export (`SetActivePowerGreaterOrEquals`) towards the heat pump, capped at its current consumption.

In both topologies the same strategy applies:

* **Elevated mode**: When the real PV surplus alone reaches the configured minimum power (the battery is never used to bridge a weak surplus over the threshold), the Controller raises the heat-pump setpoints (heating and hot-water setpoint mode, HR10000=1/HR10001 and HR10005=1/HR10006) and sets a soft power limit (HR10040=1/HR10041) to the available power, so the heat pump stores the surplus as heat. Running the heat pump on surplus is the base case, so the boost starts on the real PV surplus alone — the absence of free battery energy (support disabled, battery drained, or no prediction) does not block it. Free battery energy, when present, is not an entry precondition: it is spent cyclically during the run to ride out clouds from the battery instead of the grid (see the passive-support layer), and it stops when exhausted. Entry also requires the conditions to hold continuously for a configurable confirmation time (filters short surplus spikes); an optional forecast veto additionally requires the PV surplus alone to sustain the commit over the compressor minimum runtime (recommended only with a weather-based production predictor) — this is the place to gate entry on the forecast.
* **Compressor-cycle-aware hysteresis**: While elevated, the mode is committed for at least the minimum runtime reported by the heat pump (IR10204); after leaving, re-entry is blocked for the reported restart lock (IR10203). During the commit phase the soft power limit never drops below the minimum power, so a surplus dip cannot shut down the compressor.
* **Run extension**: When the heat pump starts a natural hot-water run on its own (storage dropped below its limit temperature), the Controller opportunistically extends the run to the elevated hot-water setpoint — the compressor is already running, so the storage is topped up without an additional compressor start. Requires full coverage (PV surplus plus deliverable battery support ≥ current heat-pump power, re-evaluated every cycle) and a minimum setpoint delta; when the free battery energy is exhausted and the surplus no longer covers the heat pump, the extension ends. The heat pump's own setpoint is latched from IR10121 while no external influence is active. Unlike a boost, the extension holds no compressor cycle, so it needs no commit-energy check.
* **Energy release and support power**: These are kept separate. The energy gate is the *free battery energy* = usable energy above Min-SoC minus the night reserve (the forecasted household deficit until the next sustained PV surplus, from the `PredictorManager` predictions, scaled by a configurable buffer). While it is positive, the battery may support the heat pump. The support *power* is not derived from this energy via an assumed duration — it is the real, currently uncovered heat-pump power, clamped by the ESS and an optional `maxBatterySupportPower` cap (0 = use the full deliverable ESS power).
* **Passive battery support**: Independent of the boost and run-extension logic, the battery pays for the heat pump's electricity whenever the pump runs on its own — capped at the spare energy above the night reserve and at the current heat-pump consumption, so nothing is taken from the household reserve or sold to the grid. If the coverage is only partial, the remainder is drawn from the grid at the heat pump's tariff. This is the base layer; the run extension builds on it by additionally raising the setpoint when the run is fully covered.

== Scheduler

This Controller must be scheduled *before* the Balancing Controller (`Controller.Symmetric.Balancing`), otherwise the forced-export constraint has no effect.

== Notes

* Without a valid prediction the Controller falls back to pure-surplus operation and raises the `NoPredictionAvailable` warning; the battery then never supports the heat pump.
* Because the heat pump is outside the balancing area, `_sum/ConsumptionActivePower` (and its prediction) covers the household only — exactly what the night reserve needs.
* Channels `ElevatedModeActive`, `FreeBatteryEnergy` (available battery energy), `EssSupportPower` (active battery support), `EssForcedExportPower` / `EssDischargeLimit` (topology-specific) and `NightReserveEnergy` document each decision for debugging and history.
