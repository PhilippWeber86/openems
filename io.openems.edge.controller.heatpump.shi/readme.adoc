= Controller SHI Heat Pump

Runs a Novelan / Alpha Innotec (SHI) heat pump preferably on PV surplus and protects the battery for the household.

== Overview

This Controller is designed for setups where the heat pump has its own, cheaper electricity tariff: PV and battery should serve the household first (household electricity is more expensive), and the heat pump may only be supplied by them when the forecast guarantees that the household stays covered.

The Controller supports both wiring topologies via the `Heat pump position` configuration. The Meter-Type of the heat pump device must be configured to match (`BEHIND_GRID_METER` -> `CONSUMPTION_METERED`, `GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER` -> `CONSUMPTION_NOT_METERED`); the Controller raises the `MeterTypeMismatch` warning otherwise.

* **`BEHIND_GRID_METER`** (default, standard installation — grid meter at the grid connection point): The heat pump is part of the grid measurement, so the Balancing Controller would serve it from the battery by default. This Controller therefore *limits* ESS discharge (`SetActivePowerLessOrEquals`) to the household demand plus the allowed support power. The surplus calculation adds the current heat-pump power back, so the heat pump does not eat its own surplus signal. If the `PredictorManager` provides a prediction for the heat-pump channel (add e.g. `heatPump0/ActivePower` to the predictor's channel addresses), it is subtracted from the consumption prediction for the night reserve; otherwise the reserve stays conservative.
* **`GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER`**: The heat pump is connected upstream of the grid meter; its consumption is invisible at the balancing point and the battery never serves it by default (fail-safe by wiring). PV surplus leaving the segment (grid export) physically flows through the heat-pump branch. While support is allowed, the Controller *forces* battery export (`SetActivePowerGreaterOrEquals`) towards the heat pump, capped at its current consumption.

In both topologies the same strategy applies:

* **Elevated mode**: When the real PV surplus alone reaches the configured minimum power (the battery is never used to bridge a weak surplus over the threshold), the Controller raises the heat-pump setpoints (heating and hot-water setpoint mode, HR10000=1/HR10001 and HR10005=1/HR10006) and sets a soft power limit (HR10040=1/HR10041) to the available power, so the heat pump stores the surplus as heat. Running the heat pump on surplus is the base case, so the boost starts on the real PV surplus alone — the absence of free battery energy (support disabled, battery drained, or no prediction) does not block it. Free battery energy, when present, is not an entry precondition: it is spent cyclically during the run to ride out clouds from the battery instead of the grid (see the passive-support layer), and it stops when exhausted. Entry also requires the conditions to hold for a configurable confirmation time in sum — the confirmation accumulates while they hold and decays faster while they do not, so short surplus spikes are filtered without a single dip resetting the progress; an optional forecast veto additionally requires the PV surplus alone to sustain the commit over the compressor minimum runtime (recommended only with a weather-based production predictor) — this is the place to gate entry on the forecast.
* **Compressor-cycle-aware hysteresis**: While elevated, the mode is committed for at least the minimum runtime reported by the heat pump (IR10204); after leaving, re-entry is blocked for the reported restart lock (IR10203). During the commit phase the soft power limit never drops below the minimum power, so a surplus dip cannot shut down the compressor. After the commit phase the boost is left only once its coverage (PV surplus plus the invited battery support) has stayed below the actual heat-pump power for a configurable switch-off delay in sum — the uncovered time accumulates and decays faster while the run is clearly covered again, with a power dead-band around the limit, so flickering clouds bridge short dips instead of either aborting the cycle on noise or keeping the boost alive indefinitely.
* **Run extension**: When the heat pump starts a natural hot-water run on its own (storage dropped below its limit temperature), the Controller opportunistically extends the run to the elevated hot-water setpoint — the compressor is already running, so the storage is topped up without an additional compressor start. Entry requires full coverage with a power margin (PV surplus plus deliverable battery support ≥ current heat-pump power + margin) and a minimum setpoint delta. As soon as the coverage drops below the heat-pump power the extension is released immediately — the SHI soft power limit is only a recommendation the heat pump may ignore, so it is not relied upon to prevent grid draw while coverage is lost. Toggling near the coverage limit is instead prevented by the entry power margin plus a re-entry lock (after a release, re-entry is blocked for the minimum switching time). The heat pump's own setpoint is latched from IR10121 while no external influence is active. Unlike a boost, the extension holds no compressor cycle, so it needs no commit-energy check.
* **Night reserve mode**: The night reserve is the battery energy kept back for the household. `MAX_DEFICIT` (default) reserves the largest cumulative household deficit over the horizon — safe, but conservative at low morning SoC because it ignores that PV will refill the battery during the day. `SOC_TRAJECTORY` simulates the battery's state of charge forward (charged by forecast PV up to the capacity, discharged by the deficit) and reserves only what that trajectory cannot spare; because midday PV refills the battery, it frees morning energy on a sunny forecast. `SOC_TRAJECTORY` is more aggressive and only reliable with a weather-based production predictor. The `Night reserve buffer` applies in both modes: in `MAX_DEFICIT` it inflates the reserve (e.g. 120 % → +20 %); in `SOC_TRAJECTORY` it becomes a cushion (a fraction of the overnight deficit) that the simulated SoC must stay above at all times — so a real safety margin remains even when the trajectory would otherwise free everything, protecting the household if the forecast recharge turns out too optimistic.
* **Battery support mode**: Governs how aggressively free battery energy drives the heat pump. `OFFENSIVE` (default) invites the battery into the heat pump's target power — the boost soft limit and the run-extension coverage are PV surplus *plus* deliverable battery power, actively turning spare energy into heat. `CLOUD_BUFFER` limits the boost and the run extension to the PV surplus *alone* (the heat pump follows the sun); the battery then only cushions short surplus dips reactively. In *both* modes a heat pump running on its own is still paid from the battery (passive support) while the night reserve holds — so e.g. an evening hot-water run is covered from spare energy even in `CLOUD_BUFFER`, but a run extension (extra heat) only happens when the sun covers it.
* **Energy release and support power**: These are kept separate. The energy gate is the *free battery energy* = usable energy above Min-SoC minus the night reserve (the forecasted household deficit until the next sustained PV surplus, from the `PredictorManager` predictions, scaled by a configurable buffer). While it is positive, the battery may support the heat pump. The support *power* is not derived from this energy via an assumed duration — it is the real, currently uncovered heat-pump power, bounded by the power the ESS can actually deliver *for the heat pump* right now (its allowed discharge power minus the share the battery already needs for the household) and an optional `maxBatterySupportPower` cap (0 = use the full deliverable ESS power). Bounding by the real remaining ESS power up front keeps the coverage checks honest: a battery that can discharge 3 kW while the household already draws 2 kW offers only 1 kW to the heat pump, so a 3 kW run is not treated as "fully covered" and the gap is not left to the grid.
* **Passive battery support**: Independent of the boost and run-extension logic, the battery pays for the heat pump's electricity whenever the pump runs on its own — capped at the spare energy above the night reserve and at the current heat-pump consumption, so nothing is taken from the household reserve or sold to the grid. If the coverage is only partial, the remainder is drawn from the grid at the heat pump's tariff. While such a self-started run is active, the soft power limit (HR10040=1/HR10041) is written to the covered power, so the heat pump can modulate down onto what PV and battery actually pay for instead of taking the difference from the grid — the setpoints stay released, because elevating them during a heating run would overheat the house. The soft limit is a recommendation: the heat pump discards it once its temperature deviates too far from its setpoint, so a genuine heat demand is protected by the device itself. The opposite case is the critical one — close to the setpoint the heat pump *does* follow the limit, and a value below its minimum sensible power would push it into a compressor stop or short cycling. The limit is therefore written exclusively while the coverage carries at least the minimum power and released entirely below that, where a "use almost nothing" recommendation would be dishonest anyway. This is the base layer; the run extension builds on it by additionally raising the hot-water setpoint when the run is fully covered.

== Scheduler

This Controller must be scheduled *before* the Balancing Controller (`Controller.Symmetric.Balancing`), otherwise the forced-export constraint has no effect.

== Notes

* Without a valid prediction the Controller falls back to pure-surplus operation and raises the `NoPredictionAvailable` warning; the battery then never supports the heat pump. An *incomplete* prediction is treated the same way: the consumption forecast must start in the current quarter and reach the required horizon (24 h). Where a quarter is missing in the Unmanaged consumption forecast, it is filled from the plain consumption channel (which includes managed consumers and is therefore &#8805; the unmanaged value — conservative); only a quarter missing in *both* channels counts as "no prediction", so a too-short or late-starting forecast still fails safe instead of releasing almost the whole battery. Missing production is filled with 0 W (also conservative). Production and consumption are aligned by time, not by array index, so an interior gap cannot misalign them.
* **Fail-safe on invalid measurements**: if the grid, ESS or heat-pump power reading is unavailable, the PV surplus cannot be verified (a missing value read as 0 W would fake a surplus). The Controller then grants no elevated mode and no battery support, releases the heat pump and leaves the ESS to the Balancing Controller, and raises the `PowerMeasurementUnavailable` warning.
* **Fail-safe on shutdown / reconfiguration**: on deactivation, on a heat-pump-ID change, and when a cycle cannot complete (e.g. the ESS component is missing), the Controller releases all external influence (heating and hot-water setpoint modes to "no influence", LPC off, PC limit 0), so a previously written setpoint elevation cannot persist on a device it no longer regulates. The heat pump's own 15-minute watchdog does not cover this, because the SHI component keeps polling and thus keeps the written values alive.
* For `GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER` the heat pump is outside the balancing area, so `_sum/ConsumptionActivePower` (and its prediction) covers the household only — exactly what the night reserve needs. For `BEHIND_GRID_METER` the heat pump is part of that measurement; its own prediction (when configured, e.g. `heatPump0/ActivePower`) is subtracted so the reserve is again based on the household load alone.
* The per-cycle `debugLog` line (`Controller.Debug.Log`) is emitted only when the `Debug Mode` option is enabled (off by default), so it does not flood the log in normal operation.
* Channels `ElevatedModeActive`, `FreeBatteryEnergy` (energy released to the heat pump; 0 when battery support is disabled), `EssSupportPower` (battery power actually supporting the heat pump), `EssForcedExportPower` / `EssDischargeLimit` (topology-specific) and `NightReserveEnergy` document each decision for debugging and history. `EssSupportPower` reflects the real support: for `GRID_SIDE_OF_GRID_METER` the forced export after ESS clamping; for `BEHIND_GRID_METER` — where the Controller only sets a discharge *allowance* — a best-effort estimate of the battery discharge actually attributable to the heat pump (0 while the battery is idle or charging), since with PV present the battery cannot be split exactly between household and heat pump.
